Not every connection to Bomber Command runs through blood. Some run through friendship — and carry just as far.

In October 2026, Stuart Lorraine contacted the Bomber Command Memorial Register about the loss of Stirling LJ930. His story was, as he put it, "an indirect one." But it is no less powerful for that.

## A friend of the family

Sergeant Kenneth Gerald Gardiner was a good friend of Stuart's parents, Jean and Dennis Lorraine. The three of them were part of the same group of young people in the Stroud area of Gloucestershire during the war.

Kenneth was killed when his aircraft was lost. He is buried in Chalford.

## The bench and the church

By coincidence — or perhaps not — Stuart's parents Jean and Dennis now have a memorial bench of their own, positioned opposite the church where Kenneth is buried. The three of them are back together.

Stuart has visited Kenneth's grave several times over the years, keeping the connection alive.

## A mother's first funeral

Stuart's mother Jean attended Kenneth's funeral. His father Dennis could not get leave from his own squadron to be there.

It was Jean's first funeral of someone she knew well. She was nineteen years old. It was a full military funeral.

In Stuart's words:

> She never liked to hear The Last Post after that.

One sentence. A lifetime of meaning.

## Paying respects

Stuart plans to visit the memorial in Surrey to pay his own respects — continuing a chain of remembrance that began with a group of friends in wartime Gloucestershire and has now reached a third generation.

These are the connections the Register exists to preserve. Not just the names and the dates, but the friendships, the grief, and the quiet acts of remembrance that carry a loss forward through the decades.

Subject: Bomber Command Memorial Register — memorial condition data that might help your work

Dear War Memorials Trust team,

I'm Keith Binley, and I run the Bomber Command Memorial Register (bombermemorial.co.uk) — a free community project documenting WW2 bomber crash site memorials across the UK. We currently have 208 memorials recorded.

I'm writing because one of the things we track for every memorial is its physical condition — rated as excellent, good, fair, or poor, with notes on specific issues. Some of these memorials are well-maintained by parish councils or local groups, but others are deteriorating, overgrown, or at risk. I thought this data might be useful to your conservation work.

Bomber crash site memorials are an unusual category. Many sit in fields, on moorland, or in remote woodland rather than in churchyards or town centres. They can be easy to overlook, and the communities nearest to them may not realise that conservation support is available. If the Trust has grant schemes or guidance that could help memorial maintainers, we'd be happy to include that information on our memorial pages — directing people to your resources when a memorial needs attention.

Every memorial on the Register includes photographs, GPS coordinates, What3Words locations, and details of who maintains it (where known). We'd be glad to share this data with you in whatever format is most useful, or to cross-reference with the War Memorials Online database.

Would you be interested in exploring how the Register might support the Trust's conservation priorities? I'm happy to start with a simple conversation or provide a data extract for review.

With best wishes,
